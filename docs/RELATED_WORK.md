# Related work and priority

The exact candidate count should be credited to Ratko Tomic, with the table as presented by Kirill Kryukov. A claim of a new result must be about sufficiency and constructive evidence, not the known numbers.

- **Kryukov (2025 update)**: [Counting chess endgames](https://kirill-kryukov.com/chess/counting-chess-endgames/). The section “Chess” is the relevant one, not the generalized (G-Chess) or arbitrary starting position (ASP-Chess) sections. It gives the capture and promotion restrictions and the standard-chess table. It also distinguishes “endgames” (material types) from endgame side-to-move classes. Its numerical table is public-domain (CC0).
- **Tomic (1999)**: *Counting & Encoding Any Chess Position in 157 bits*, [archived URL](https://www.stmintz.com/ccc/index.php?id=77068); *Algorithm for Encoding Any Position in 155 bits (part 2/2)*, [archived URL](https://www.stmintz.com/ccc/index.php?id=77859). Both are referenced by Kryukov. The full original messages were not recovered. Their titles concern coding, for which a necessary condition alone suffices, but that observation does not establish what the messages proved. Do not claim that the original author did not prove sufficiency.
- **Hans Havermann (1999)**: early generalized counting formula, as credited by Kryukov. This is a count of weak compositions modulo color interchange without the standard initial material constraints.
- **Infuehr, Blass, Muller et al. (2022)**: [Number of unique piece counts in chess](https://talkchess.com/viewtopic.php?t=80375). Discussion of 8,694 possible one-side profiles (75,585,636 ordered pairs under separate side constraints); the latter is a weaker candidate space than Tomic's.
- **Chess Stack Exchange (2014)**: [How many possible combinations for the number of pieces on the board?](https://chess.stackexchange.com/questions/4561/how-many-possible-combinations-for-the-number-of-pieces-on-the-board). The question aggregates piece types across both colors, unlike our two-sided count; its unresolved aspects do not demonstrate novelty of our theorem.
- **Gourion (2022)**: [An upper bound for the number of chess diagrams without promotion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09386), ICGA Journal, DOI [10.3233/ICG-220210](https://doi.org/10.3233/ICG-220210). A bound on diagrams, using pawn-arrangement classes, rather than an exact characterization of material profiles.
- **Tromp and contributors**: [ChessPositionRanking](https://github.com/tromp/ChessPositionRanking), a ranking and estimation project for legal positions, supported by concrete proof games and illegality proofs. [Texel's proof-game documentation](https://github.com/peterosterlund2/texel/blob/master/doc/proofgame.md) describes proof-game search and kernels. Both provide related methodologies, not evidence of priority for our exact material theorem.
- **RemcoGerlich (2013)**: [Maximum number of queens possible](https://chess.stackexchange.com/questions/4123/maximum-number-of-queens-possible), containing a constructive example of 18 queens. This shows that extreme promotions are not novel in themselves. Our included example with 14 queens and 28 pieces serves as a certificate illustration, not a separate priority claim.
- **FIDE (2023)**: [Laws of Chess](https://handbook.fide.com/chapter/E012023). The definition of reachable legal positions and automatic game termination are central to witness soundness.

## Unresolved priority question

Searches for the exact candidate count, material configurations, piece counts, Tomic's message titles, and proof-game methods did not identify an independently published complete sufficiency proof. This is evidence of a search outcome, not proof of absence. Kryukov's text presents the count as standard chess endgames and does not explicitly label it an upper bound. The original Tomic messages may contain an argument that settles priority. For a publication, contact Kryukov (contact given on his page) and, if feasible, Tomic and other chess-combinatorics researchers. State precisely that the candidate count is known and that the repository supplies explicit constructive certificates; do not assert “first proof” without stronger evidence.
